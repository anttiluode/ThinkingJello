THE NEXT SMALL THING

A body that knows its own echo.

Every launch splits in two: a pulse travels through the pond, and an internal copy enters a bank of delayed traces. Learned weights predict the four local sensor returns. The prediction is made before the current return arrives. Their difference is the residual.

The body’s glowing fibres show learned delay weights. Its membrane and internal currents respond to sensor activity. Unexpected energy lights the amber interior; a simple curiosity rule can turn the body toward the local residual gradient. Moving also changes the echo it needs to learn.

Try this: wait for the echo to become familiar, freeze learning, then move a stone. The old expectation persists while the actual return changes. Restore learning and watch the gap close. Switch off the internal copy: even its own pulses become unexplained.

Three bands, three sets of weights. This separation is supplied by the design. It makes selective interference testable; it does not claim to solve JelloBrain’s shared-material problem through emergence.

Room mode uses quiet speaker bursts and three microphone energy bands with the same adaptive filter. It learns an envelope response, not a full acoustic waveform or a speech model. Headphones, latency, room noise and browser processing affect the result. The phase portrait is a visualization, not proof of a Takens embedding.
